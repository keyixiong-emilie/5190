Can we do RLHF without RL?

Two ways to align a toy language model with human preferences. Route 1 fits rewards with a Bradley–Terry model, then applies the closed-form KL-optimal policy. Route 2 (DPO) skips the reward model and trains the policy directly. Watch them arrive at the same policy, then see the one case where neither has a finite answer.

The toy language model

    What the two routes compute

    Route 1 · Bradley–Terry reward fit + closed-form policy

    Fit rewards r by logistic regression on the pairs (Bradley–Terry):

    LR(r) = − mean log σ( rw − rl )

    Then use the closed-form optimum of the KL-regularized objective:

    π*(y) ∝ πref(y) · exp( r(y) / β )

    Route 2 · DPO

    Skip the reward model. Run gradient descent on the policy logits θ:

    LDPO(θ) = − mean log σ( β log[πθ(yw)/πref(yw)] − β log[πθ(yl)/πref(yl)] )

    With r = β(θ − log πref) this is the same loss as Route 1. With six free logits and the same data, πref and β, the two share an optimal policy whenever a finite best-fit reward exists.

    Things to try

    1. Press Play training. The routes take different paths and meet at the same policy.
    2. Stop on the Policy chart at β = 1. Response A has the highest reward, yet C is still the most likely answer. The policy is πref reweighted by exp(r/β), and πref starts C at 30% and A at 5%. Drag β down to 0.1 and A climbs to about 98%.
    3. While you drag β, watch the Reward chart. It does not move. β changes how reward turns into a policy, not the reward that is learned.
    4. Tick Match step sizes and play again. One DPO step of size η moves the implicit reward by η·β², so with ηθ = ηr/β² the two paths coincide at every step.
    5. Switch to Response A always wins. The reward margin has no finite optimum and keeps growing. For any fixed β the policy drifts toward “always answer A”, and a larger β keeps it closer to πref at any given step.

    About step sizes. Route 1 uses ηr = 2. Route 2 uses ηθ = 0.5·ηr/β² (or ηr/β² when matched). Scaling by 1/β² makes the implicit reward move at the same pace for every β, which is why the reward-margin curve does not change when you drag β. With one fixed ηθ, a larger β would take larger steps in reward space.
